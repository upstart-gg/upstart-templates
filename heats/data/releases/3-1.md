The tokenizer shipped in 3.0 cut Hangul syllables in two whenever a line began with a number. It cost Korean readers a fifth of their context, and it took us seven weeks to see it, because none of our evals starts a line with a number. Jae-won Park found it in a bug report about a grocery list.

Fixing a tokenizer means training again from the last checkpoint that did not depend on it. We went back 400 billion tokens, and the two training logs are side by side in the repository.

Flock’s 4-bit file took longer than its training. Sixty-four experts do not round well: each one needed its own scale. The method is in section 4 of the report.

## Before you upgrade

If you fine-tuned 3.0, your adapter still loads on 3.1, but its Korean and Swahili will be slightly off. Train it again with the new tokenizer; it takes the same time as before.
