Danny Phommachanh opened the second Kettle Ten in 2024, across town from the first. Six of his cooks work in both.

“For three months I had two whiteboards and a car,” he says. “Whatever I wrote in one kitchen was wrong in the other by the time I got there.”

## One week for both

The week now shows both kitchens, and a cook who works in both appears once, with each shift in its place. Nobody is booked in two kitchens at the same hour; the schedule won’t let it happen.

## The end of the night

Danny approves the day’s hours from his phone after close, looking only at what’s flagged: an early start, a break not taken. “Five minutes, and I’m done. It used to be my Monday morning.”
