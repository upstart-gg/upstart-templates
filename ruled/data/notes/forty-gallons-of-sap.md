At the end of March, the hills east of here smoke. From the road it looks like trouble. It’s sugaring: people standing up all night beside a steel pan, boiling forty gallons of sap down to one of syrup.

The first time I watched it, I asked the man at the pan whether it bothered him, all that steam. He looked at me as if I’d asked whether it bothered him that the syrup was sweet.

Week four of the course is named after that pan. You bring your draft, which by now is about as long as it wants to be, and you cut it by half. You keep what you cut, in a file with a friendly name. Then you put back what you miss.

## What people find out

Most of them put back three sentences. Almost nobody puts back a paragraph. One writer put back a single word, *still*, and it was the right word, and the essay is better with it standing there on its own.

The other thirty-nine gallons were not wasted. They were how you got it hot enough.
