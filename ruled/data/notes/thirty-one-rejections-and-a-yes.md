The title essay of *The Lower Falls* was turned down thirty-one times. I know because I kept the slips, and later the emails, printed, in a shoebox that once held snow boots.

Nine of them were addressed to somebody else. Two came for essays I hadn’t sent. One said the editors had read it “with interest and, in one case, alarm.”

## What the shoebox says

I went through it last winter, looking for wisdom, and found mostly arithmetic. The essay changed four times in those thirty-one tries. After each change, the notes got longer and more particular. By the twenties, people were telling me which paragraph they had argued about.

The yes came on a Tuesday in February, from a magazine I had nearly left off the list because I liked it too much.

## What I tell people in week six

Send it to three places. Choose them because you read them. Write a cover letter of four sentences. Put the date in a notebook, and start something else that same afternoon.

Then go out to the mailbox when you feel like a walk, and not before.
